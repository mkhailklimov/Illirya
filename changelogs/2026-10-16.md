### 1. Less card-opening, more happening
Dice on risky advice, letters from Rome, the tribes, a town you can watch grow, and the quake (below) give each season something to decide or watch.

### 2. Earthquakes: rare and dramatic
They looked rare on paper (2% of the land deck) but in winter only five land cards can come, so the real chance was 5–8%. Now: 1%, never twice within four years, about once in two governorships. When it comes the card shakes with a rumble, 50–100 people die, a building in town is thrown down (rebuilt in two seasons), 600 denarii, 10 timber, loyalty −5, and no newcomers that season.

### 3. Peace makes the town grow
Every season without a calamity, with bread and loyalty 40+, people grow 1.5% (2% when content), up to the houses; the council now asks for houses when the town is nearly full ("more room, more taxpayers"). More people eat more and pay more tax.

### 4. Dice
- Every **risky** proposal on a card is now a gamble: the big d20 + the advisor's talent (his six qualities are finally used), need 10, with what you win or lose written on the proposal before you choose.
- Treaties and battles with the tribes are d20 rolls, and Antistius can lead the charge.
- Ships still roll for the voyage home (in the corner die).

### 5. The compass
Redrawn as a classical eight-point wind rose: slender two-tone points, a ring of ticks, north in crimson, and the wind as a glowing arc and three streaks blowing in from its side (no more cartoon head).

### 6. The city grows on the map
- Small roofs fill the streets inside the walls, one for every 120 people.
- A finished building glows and says NEW for a season; building sites are hatched.
- A band of tribal land runs across the top of the city sheet: three territories with their plots, locked until won; they turn into fields when you win them.

### 7. History: letters from Rome
Seven letters arrive as the sea card on their real dates, each a decision: the fall of Sejanus (Autumn 31), the credit crisis (Summer 33), Tiberius dies and Caligula succeeds (Spring 37), Caligula wants gold (Summer 39), Caligula demands worship (Summer 40), Caligula murdered and Claudius (Winter 40), the revolt of Scribonianus, governor of Dalmatia (Spring 42), as the finale. The decree "honouring Tiberius" now names the reigning emperor. The governorship ends after Winter AD 42 with a score.

### 8. The tribes beyond the fields
Docleatae (the valley inland), Ardiaei (Teuta's old people, in the hills) and Pirustae (the iron mountains). Two ways to their land: **diplomacy and gold** (gifts, a d20 with Nikandros's tongue; failures make the next try easier) or **sword and fire** (Antistius, 150+ men; victory brings land and loot but years of raids). Click a tribe on the city sheet to see both ways with the real odds. Nikandros and Antistius propose them when the fields are full.

### 9. The fourth order
The wall of buttons is gone. Instead: **three prepared choices** from the council, and **a prompt window**: the scribe now answers anything (questions too: "how do I get money without raising taxes?") using Claude, on your own account, when the page is allowed to ask; otherwise the old word matcher. The first time, claude.ai asks you to allow it. "All actions…" opens the full list for when you want it. The same AI scribe answers in the "your own order" box on each card.

### The strategy math (economy early, army late)
The new bot simulation (four styles, 40 games each, to AD 42) found that loyalty drained to nothing in almost every game: one early tax rise cost −4 loyalty every season forever and nobody ever lowered it. Fixed (taxes, peace and bread, advisors proposing lower taxes and houses), then:

| Style | Finish AD 42 | Score | People at the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economy first, army late | 24 / 40 | 426 | ~6,250 |
| Always cautious | 19 / 40 | 367 | ~5,700 |
| Random | 2 / 40 | 213 | |
| Army first | 0 / 40 (famine) | 218 | |

So the wise governor's path now wins, and an army-first start starves.

## Evening: rules v5, three dice decide the season (version 9, same link)

Your idea, as built: decisions first, fortune after.
- **Three decisions, then three dice.** Each card is decided in turn, but nothing happens yet. When all three are decided, the dice are thrown together: **opal for the land, aquamarine for the sea, onyx for the gods** (new stones in `design/source/dice.py`). Each lands as **disaster (1), setback, success or triumph (20)**, and only then are the orders carried out, well or badly.
- **Every card rolls.** Cautious advice has a low DC (6) and narrow stakes (a setback costs a quarter more); risky advice has DC 11 and wide stakes (a convoy +30%, or half the cargo lost; a building in half the time, or dearer and late); your own order DC 9; "let it be" DC 9 on a bad card (a setback makes it half as bad again) or 4 on a good one. A disaster on a sea order sinks the ship with her cargo, the money and ten marines; on a building, the works collapse.
- **The odds, shown the Baldur's Gate way:** every choice shows a strip of the twenty faces coloured by what they mean, the chance in per cent, the die, the DC, and each bonus by name: the advisor's talent and mood, the wind for sea orders ("the bora is rising −3", "a flat calm +3", winter −3), the sages' forecast +1, walls, barracks and garrison for arms, the temple for omens, a sullen or grieving town −1. Your own order shows its check before you confirm it.
- **The fourth order comes after the dice** and knows how they fell: the scribe's prompt includes the season's outcomes, so you can ask it to make good a loss or follow up a success.
- **The corner** shows the three dice of the last throw.
- Balance: with the town growing, harvests (counted per head) fell behind and famine killed most governors; each wheat terrace now feeds about 15% more. Bot simulation, 40 games each: economy first 25 finish (score 449), always cautious 26 (427), random 6 (236), army first 0 (all famine). Across a game the card dice land about 78% success, 11% setback, 5% disaster, 5% triumph.
