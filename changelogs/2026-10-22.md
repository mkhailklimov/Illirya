### 1. Battles with consequences
- **Missiles fire in stages, and the enemy's formation matters.** Slingers throw twice, far and near. Their stones fly far but wide, so they do best against a dense crowd: 16% against the massed tribes, but only 9–10% against scattered pirates or brigands. Archers shoot once, nearer, but aimed: 10% against anyone. On the battlefield the pirates and brigands now stand scattered and the tribes in a mass. The battle log shows each stage (far, near, javelins).
- **Slingers and archers need a shield line.** In a lane with no cohort, allies or horsemen, they count −2, and when the enemy closes they are cut down: at least 45% of them in a won lane, 80% in a lost one. The order of battle warns you in red. The default deployment always keeps them behind a cohort.
- **The village militia's dead are the town's dead:** they come off the people.
- **Spoils.** If you beat the pirates you may take one of their ships: d20 ≥ 12, +4 if your horsemen pursue. She is renamed and joins your fleet, or is sold for 500 if no berth is free. If you beat the tribes at home they may flee and leave a vineyard or an olive grove on the border (d20 ≥ 13): a new field plot, already bearing.
- **Refill the cohorts** (new order, in the garrison): townsmen take the places of the fallen, up to the army's best strength (100 at most at once). Each one costs 5 denarii (6 with javelins), a shield (1 timber per 10 men) and iron for his weapons (1 per 10 men for each: spear, gladius, pila). The town loses as many people. Antistius proposes it when 30 or more are missing.

### 2. Justice, discontent and rebellion
- **After every victory you judge the defeated: execute or pardon.** Executing pleases Rome (favour +1) but frightens the town (discontent +2). Pardoning wins hearts (loyalty +2, discontent −3). Hanging brigands in the forum counts as an execution.
- **Your name as a judge.** After three judgements the town makes up its mind. If you pardon fewer than 15% you are a cruel tyrant (discontent +2 every season). At 20–40% you are stern but just (−1 a season); one in three or four is the sweet spot. Above 50% you are a weak ruler (+2 a season, and brigands come three times as often).
- **A new number: Discontent** (0–100, starts at 10). Click it in the ledger to see why it moved. **What raises it:** high taxes (+3 a season, +6 when you raise them), hunger (+6), little bread (+2), low loyalty (+2), executions and conscription. **What lowers it:** festivals (−8), lower taxes (−6), petitions (−4), the temple (−2), pardons, peace and bread, and a loyal town. A warning appears from 35.
- **Above 50 the town rises.** You may parley (d20 + loyalty/10, need 15): taxes fall to low, and Rome thinks you weak. Or you fight. Half the slingers and archers you raised from the townsmen go over to the rebels. The rebels are thousands strong but untrained, and they fight in the streets, where the walls are no help. **If you lose, you are overthrown. If you win, half the people are dead, half the goods and the chest are lost, two buildings are thrown down, and Rome asks how it came to this (favour −8).**

### 3. The fleet, the voyages, the invention
- **Every ship has a name** (Fortuna, Concordia, Aquila, Victoria...), given when her keel is laid. The slipway on the city plan shows her name and the seasons left.
- **Announcements:** a ship launched, a voyage home, a prize taken, a new invention. Each shows as a notice at the bottom of the screen and in the report's achievements.
- **Each voyage keeps a ledger:** what she sold and for how much, the goods she brought back (valued at their price there), what she cost at the outset, and the **profit or loss**. The notice says it in one line: "The Fortuna is home from Aquileia: profit +640 denarii."
- **The Adriatic map shows each ship on her route,** as a galley with a striped square sail, oars and the painted eye on the bow. She heads out, then home, labelled with her name; the ships in harbour lie moored in the bay.
- **The fleet panel** under the map shows every ship, in harbour, at sea (where, with what, home when) or on the slipway, with the ledger of the last six voyages.
- **Inventions:** the screw press (the Greek engineer's sea card) is now a lasting machine: **olive oil +30% at every harvest, and +50 denarii every season** from neighbours who press their olives with it. The city sheet has an Inventions panel, and every season's report shows what it brought.

## Evening: rules v10 (version 14 at the same link)

Your second list, built.

### Acruvium as an old map
The city sheet is now drawn as a parchment map in sepia ink. It has hachured little mountains, water lining along the bay, double-line rivers in verdigris, carmine roofs and towers, and a title cartouche. There is also an ink compass rose, a sea serpent in the gulf, a scale of passus and a chequered border. The tribes' lands sit above the ridge with dash-dot boundaries. Plots, scrub and tribal land work as before.

### The priest is useful now
Sacrifice at the temple, and Tertius Volusius reads the liver:
- **Mars:** who comes next, in which season, and whether they will storm the walls or raid the fields, with Antistius's odds as things stand. Forewarned, the men fight that battle at +1.
- **Neptune:** every ship at sea gets +2 on her voyage home.
- **Ceres:** a warning when the granaries run low.
- **The household gods:** a warning when loyalty nears 50.
- **One piece of advice:** a concrete order. Bread comes first, then the coming war (if the odds are poor), then the town's mood, else the best of the council's suggestions. He repeats it at the council table next season.

A malformed liver (the die's setback) means the gods are silent.

### Walls or fields
- Each year's raiders either **storm the town** or **raid the fields**. Pirates come to the Sea Gate one time in two. The tribes come against the walls two times in five. Brigands always come in the open.
- **Against the walls,** the walls and the citadel count. Archers and slingers shoot from the walls, and the attackers bunch at the gate, so the slings do better. Lose, and they break in: a quarter of the chest, and a building thrown down.
- **In the fields, the walls cannot help.** You fight on your own ground (+1), and an empty lane is held only by the farmers (−1). Or you **shut the gates:** nobody of ours falls, but they burn a field building, take a month of grain, the farmers curse you (loyalty −4) and Rome hears you hid (favour −2).
- **Slingers and archers without a shield line** are still cut down (since v9), and the order of battle warns you in red.
- **Pirates beaten at the quay** give up a ship more easily (d20 ≥ 10; 12 on the beach).

### One number: loyalty
Discontent is gone. **If loyalty falls below 50, the town is restless. If it is still below 50 at the end of the next season, it rises in rebellion.** You get one full season of orders to win it back. Loyalty starts at 60. Click Loyalty in the ledger to see what moved it and your name as a judge. A pardon gives +3 loyalty; an execution −2. The advisors no longer push taxes up below loyalty 68.
