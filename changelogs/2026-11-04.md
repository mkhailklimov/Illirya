## Version 24 (rules v17): fewer words, brighter cards, a trumpet, and the tribes' trap

### Fewer words, more pictures
Your wife is right, so the long sentences are gone from the main path:
- **First screen:** picture tiles (turn 3 cards, take advice, throw the dice, give an order; the four ways to win). The rules are under "How to play".
- **Cards:** a much bigger picture and the title. What a card does is shown as icons with numbers, not a sentence.
- **Decisions:** each advisor offers an icon, a price and a coloured chance bar. His words are in the tooltip, and "Your own order" is folded away.
- **Letters and choices:** one line of story, the rest under "More". Each option shows icons with numbers.
- **Orders:** no speech; the scribe is folded away.
- **Battle:** a call to arms shows "1505 against 350 + 110" and buttons. The order of battle is the map and "130 : 110" per lane. Buttons and rules are folded. Antistius gives his map and two lines, the rest under "What else he says".
- **City, army, top line, tabs:** icons and short labels instead of paragraphs. The council panel shows remarks only when an advisor has something to say.

### Brighter cards
The card to turn now pulses and grows, with a bright sweep across it and a strong glow in its deck's colour: gold for Land, blue for Sea, magenta for Omen. The other cards go nearly dark. The card you are deciding on grows and glows the same way. The pictures sit on stronger colours. The thirteen cards added last week have their own pictures now.

### The trumpet
Turning a Land card now sounds a military trumpet call: four brass notes rising, with an echo off the mountain. It is far louder and higher than the old low horn call, which laptop speakers barely played.

### The three tribes, and the trap
There are three tribes: the **Labeatae on the coast** (they raid by boat), the **Docleatae** and the **Pirustae** inland.

When your fields run out, their land tempts you, and your council may even suggest it. But:
- **Seize a tribe that has done you no wrong:** you win easily. You get the land and the loot, but the villagers flee. A year later **the hordes** come from every valley, more than you can stop, and take it all back. They burn fields and kill people, and Rome blames you. A strong army of 300 with engines holds about one time in eight.
- **The right way:** wait for a tribe to attack, beat them off, and **show mercy**. They may ask to **join Acruvium**, and then their land and all their people are yours, without another battle. Gifts and repeated defeats make this likelier.
- **If they do not join,** you now have **just cause** (it shows on the map). You can prepare a war: a harder fight, but no hordes, and the land and half their people stay yours.
- **Their villages then fight beside you** whenever you are attacked. Land seized without cause sends no one.

Gifts no longer buy land: they bring goodwill, which helps a tribe decide to join.

### A real map of the villages
The boxes above the city are now a map of the hinterland, joined to the bay. It has the coast and the sea, the river Zeta, the Labeatan lake, mountains, tracks between the villages, and borders for each tribe. Each village has its own place and name (Butua, Olcinium, Scodra, Doclea, Anderva and others) and shows as a palisade with roofs until it is yours, then as a plot you can build on. Each tribe's border and label show whether it is free, hostile, joined or conquered, or whether you have just cause.

- **In the browser:** a tribe's attack with mercy (just cause recorded), and the hordes taking back seized land, ran with no script errors. The "ask to join" pop-up itself was tested only in the engine test, not on screen.
