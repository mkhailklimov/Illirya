### 5. The dice: four throws, each with a meaning
Before, the battle was three rounds of the same three dice, and it was not clear why. Now it follows your order:

1. **Our engines.** One die for each scorpion and ballista, and only three engines at most take the field (ballistae first). No engines, no throw. 10 or more is a hit: a ballista kills a tenth of the crowd in its lane, a scorpion a fourteenth. A 20 does half again, a 1 jams.
2. **Our slingers and archers.** One die for each lane where they stand, up to three. 10 or more strikes home, below 10 the volley scatters (less effect), and a 1 is wasted. **Slingers do more damage the denser the enemy**, as with the massed tribes. **Archers aim**, so they do better against a scattered enemy (pirates, brigands) and a little less against a dense mass.
3. **The enemy's charge.** Their die for each lane, with their fury and against our strength there (numbers, discipline, walls, veterans, the governor...). 10 or more breaks into our line: we lose men and heart. Below 10, our line holds and they lose men and heart.
4. **Our counter-attack.** Our die for each lane where our line still stands, with our strength. 10 or more throws them back hard, below 10 we are thrown back.

A side whose heart runs out in a lane **runs**. After the four throws, each lane still undecided goes to whoever kept the better heart. **Two lanes of three win the day.** The second line still steps in when the front wavers.

The dice screen shows each throw in turn, with its title and a line saying what it means. You press "Throw 3 dice" for each throw, and every die says what happened ("14 − 3 = 11: they break into our line").

### 1. No damage hints
The order of battle no longer shows "after missiles" or the percentages each volley will kill. You see how many men stand against how many, the bonuses, the ground and the chance to win. What the slingers and archers actually do is known only when their dice fall.

### 2. Shooters must be hidden
Slingers and archers must stand **behind a cohort** (or allies, or horsemen) in their lane, or **on the city walls**. Left alone, they are caught by the charge **on a 4 or more**, so 85% of the time: they are cut down (nine in ten of them die) and **the lane is lost with them**. The order of battle warns you in red. In a test against pirates, archers behind a cohort held their lane 87% of the time; alone they held it 9%.

### 3. Enemy formations
The small dots are gone. The enemy now stands in **shaped formations**: blocks of shields in their colour with a row of spear points facing us. The tribes come in **one deep dense mass** per lane, bigger the more they are. Pirates and brigands come in **several loose bands** spread across the lane. Pirates are sea-blue, brigands earth-brown, tribes red, rebels purple. When a lane breaks, its formation fades and falls back.

### 4. The ground
Every battle has its own ground: **up to two of rocks, a wood, a brook or a low hill**, in random lanes, plus harmless trees and stones for variety. Each matters, and its label is on the map:
- **A brook:** they must wade it, their charge −2.
- **Rocks:** they break their ranks, their charge −1; our horsemen cannot strike there.
- **A wood:** stones and arrows lose half their force there.
- **A hill:** we hold the high ground, +1 to us, and our missiles carry further.

### Balance
The odds against each enemy are close to before. With 150 men, against the tribes at the walls you win about 72% of the time (was 77%), in the fields 67% (was 61%); against pirates 82–91%, brigands 92%. The bot simulation still finishes 9 of 20 games economy-first. The bots win fewer of their battles (58–81%), mostly because a bot never moves its shooters; the shown odds and the results agree.

## Later: three throws instead of four (version 18)

At your suggestion, the engines and the shooters now throw together. A battle is **three throws, one die for each lane (left, centre, right)**:
1. **Our engines, slingers and archers** shoot together in each lane where any of them stand. The die's card says who is there ("2 engines, 3 bands of shooters"). Each engine adds its weight to its lane's volley; one with no enemy in front shoots at the biggest crowd. Three engines at most still take the field.
2. **Their charge.**
3. **Our counter-attack.**

The odds are unchanged (with 150 men: tribes at the walls 68%, in the fields 64%; pirates 82–92%; brigands 94%). Checked in the browser: the three throws run, with no script errors.
