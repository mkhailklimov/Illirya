## Advisor pictures: prompts version 3

Your ChatGPT drafts of the priest and the general (now kept in `design/pieces/figures/drafts-too-detailed/`) were again a modern illustration about Rome rather than an ancient painting:
- a dark background with a glow;
- engraved hatching everywhere;
- a realistic face turned three-quarters;
- muscles and ornament on every edge.

The prompts are rewritten in [design/prompts/advisor-figures.md](../design/prompts/advisor-figures.md):
- **The target is now an Etruscan tomb painting from Tarquinia** (the Tomb of the Leopards, already in `references/`): cream plaster, skin in one flat red-brown, a wobbly brush outline, a few inner lines, a strict profile with a big almond eye, stiff naive bodies.
- **Each prompt opens with "a photograph of an ancient wall painting... not a modern illustration".** The tool then copies an object, not a mood.
- **One shared palette with hex codes.** Each advisor has one clothing colour: priest dark red, general ochre, fleet blue, economist green, treasurer white with a red border.
- **The detail-inviting words are gone:** no fringes, breastplate ornament, helmet crests, painted eyes or keys. Objects are described as simple shapes ("a liver, a rounded brown blob").
- **Five complete prompts, one per advisor**, each in its own block, style included, ready to paste.
- **A ChatGPT routine:**
  1. Start a new chat for each figure.
  2. Attach the tomb photo and have it describe the style in five points.
  3. Paste the prompt.
  4. Correct the result step by step with ready fix lines ("remove all hatching", "half as many lines").
  5. Once one figure is right, use it as the reference for the other four.
- **The vase look stays as an alternative** (two replacement paragraphs).

The game is unchanged; still version 19 (rules v14).

## Later: the top bar, and the battle as a campaign map (version 20)

### The top bar
Loyalty was drawn as a grey button, unlike its neighbours. It now looks like every other number (click it, as before, for what moves it). A number still turns red when it is in danger (grain below 3 months, loyalty near rebellion, favour below 30).

### The battlefield, drawn like a historical battle map
After the maps you sent (the Trebia, Cannae, Ilerda, Formigny):
- **The look:**
  - flat sand ground, with hills as soft rings of darker sand;
  - pale blue water, green groves and grey rocks;
  - farm plots when they raid the fields, the sea and their boats when pirates land;
  - **Rome in red, the enemy in blue.**
- **Signs on the blocks, as on those maps:**
  - **Cohorts** are red blocks with white stripes; allies are orange.
  - **Slingers and archers** are light blocks with a red cross (archers with a little bow, village levies dashed).
  - **Horsemen** have a single diagonal bar.
  - **Engines** show a dot (a ballista two).
  - **The enemy** comes either as a solid blue block (a dense mass) or as small blue light-troop blocks with a cross (loose bands). **Dashed blue arrows** show their advance.
- **A title box** with the place, season and year, and their numbers. **A legend**, a **scale in passus**, a **north arrow**, and the flank names along the bottom edge.
- **The ground parts the flanks.** Between left and centre, and between centre and right, runs **a stream, a grove, rocks or a ravine**: a different pair every battle, named at the bottom of the map. They do not change the dice; they show that each flank fights its own fight. The ground features inside a lane (rocks, a wood, a brook, a low hill) still count as before, now drawn in the same map style and labelled under the enemy's numbers.
- The walls, when they storm the town, are a dark line with square towers; the standard is a small red flag.

### No more luck percentages
Every win chance is gone:
- from the call to arms ("Send Antistius", "Lead them yourself");
- from the order of battle (the total and each lane's "% to hold");
- from the "march on the tribe" button.

What you still see is the facts: men against men, the ground, the bonuses by name, and the warning when slingers or archers stand without shields. The judgement is yours.

### Antistius advises, you decide
When you lead the battle yourself, **Antistius first says how he would deploy**, in a full-size pop-up:
- where their main strength is and how many cohorts he would put there;
- whether slingers or archers suit this enemy;
- what the walls, the fields and the ground mean;
- which streams or groves part the flanks;
- where to put the engines and the horsemen;
- a warning about unprotected shooters.

His plan is drawn on a small map beside his words. Then:
- **Deploy as he advises**: his plan is on the field, and you can still move every block.
- **I will deploy them myself**: the army stands in marching order along the back of the field, and you place everything.

You can call him again with "Ask Marcus Antistius" on the order of battle.
